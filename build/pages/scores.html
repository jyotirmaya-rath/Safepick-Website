How scores work

Honest about what a score can and can't tell you.

SafePick reads what is printed on a pack and turns it into a score out of 100 using published rules. Here are the rules, the data behind them, and where a score stops being useful.

One number, three verdicts.

Every scored product gets a number from 0 to 100 and one of three words. The same bands are used on every screen of the app.

{{> bands}}
  • Good, 70 to 100. The label shows little to worry about for this kind of product.
  • Okay, 50 to 69. Some concerns to be aware of.
  • Avoid, 0 to 49. Several concerns. Look at the better choices.
  • Never colour alone. Each verdict has a number, a colour, a word and a face, so it reads the same for everyone.

What goes into a food score.

Food and drink scores compare what the label declares, per 100 g or 100 ml, with reference ranges and a few category adjustments.

  • Energy
  • Added sugar
  • Total sugar
  • Saturated fat
  • Trans fat
  • Sodium
  • Protein
  • Fibre
  • Ingredient list

Drinks are judged without protein and fibre, because a drink isn't expected to supply them. Beauty and personal-care scores use the ingredient list only.

The wording always describes the label, like "high in sodium per 100 g". It never says what a food will do to you.

Missing data is never guessed

Nutrients a pack doesn't declare are left out and shown as "Not on label". With too little data there is no number at all, and partial data caps the score at 59, so it can't read Good.

Some products aren't scored

Baby food and infant formula, supplements, alcoholic drinks, and basics like salt, spices, plain tea, coffee and water. The page says why instead of showing a number that wouldn't mean much.

"Why this score?" on every product

The nutrient and ingredient flags, the positives, the version of the method, the data source and the date the data was last updated.

Scores can't be bought

No brand, store or partner can pay to change a score, a ranking or the better-choices list. Store links are added after the score, never before.

A barcode proves identity, not the pack

A recognised barcode confirms which product this is in our data. It can't confirm that the pack in your hand is genuine or in date.

Where the method stands today

The reference ranges are simplified and have not yet been reviewed by an independent nutritionist. The app's About screen always shows the current review status.

Where the data comes from.

More than a million products sold in Canada, the USA and India. Each product page names its source and the date its data was last updated.

  • Open databases

    Open Food Facts and Open Beauty Facts, two public product databases available under the Open Database License. They supply most of the catalogue.

  • Community submissions

    People add missing products by photographing the pack and typing the label. Nothing is shown to anyone else until a reviewer has compared the typed values with the photos. These records are marked "Community-submitted".

  • Brand submissions

    Brands can send their own label data. It is checked the same way and marked "Brand-submitted". Neither kind of submission is presented as an independent audit. How brands send data.

  • Corrections

    Anyone can use "Report a problem" on a product page. A reviewer looks at each report, and every change is logged so it can be undone.

Not medical advice. SafePick reads what's printed on a pack and turns it into a score and plain notes. It never replaces the label or advice from your doctor, dietitian, pharmacist or dermatologist, especially for allergies, diabetes, pregnancy, children and skin conditions.
