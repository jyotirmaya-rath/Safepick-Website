There is no official ranking of foods for children, and any list of ten is a simplification. This one collects the everyday foods that Canada's food guide and children's nutrition guidance return to again and again: foods that supply iron, calcium, fibre, protein and healthy fats without much added sugar or salt. None of them is exotic, and most are cheap.

The advice is for children aged two and up. Babies and toddlers under two have different needs, so follow your doctor's or public-health nurse's guidance for them.

  1. Vegetables of every colour

    Dark green ones like broccoli and spinach, and orange ones like carrots and sweet potato, bring iron, folate, vitamin A and fibre. Fresh, frozen and canned all count.

    Serving tip: for children under four, cook hard vegetables until soft or grate them. Big pieces of raw carrot are a choking risk.

  2. Whole fruit

    Berries, apples, bananas, oranges. Whole fruit comes with fibre that slows the sugar down. Juice doesn't: even with no sugar added, fruit juice counts toward the free sugars the WHO says to limit.

    Serving tip: cut grapes and other small round fruit into quarters for young children, and take out pits.

  3. Eggs

    A protein food that is quick to cook and easy to chew. The yolk is a source of iron, which young children need a lot of for their size.

  4. Plain yogurt

    Protein and calcium in a form most children like. The word that matters is plain: flavoured yogurts can carry a lot of added sugar.

    Serving tip: buy plain and add your own fruit. Compare the sugars line on two tubs and the difference is easy to see.

  5. Oats and other whole grains

    Oatmeal, whole grain cereal, brown rice, whole grain pasta. Whole grains keep the fibre and minerals that refining removes.

    Label check: many children's cereals are high in sugars. Look for more fibre and less sugar per serving.

  6. Beans, peas and lentils

    Plant-based protein with iron and fibre, and one of the cheapest foods in the store. Canada's food guide suggests choosing protein foods that come from plants more often.

    Serving tip: mash them into soups, sauces or dips like hummus. Rinse canned beans to wash off some of the salt.

  7. Fish

    Protein and iron, and fatty fish such as salmon and trout supply the unsaturated fats young children need for energy and growth.

    Serving tip: check carefully for bones before serving to young children.

  8. Nut and seed butters

    Peanut, almond or sunflower seed butter gives protein and unsaturated fat in a spoonful.

    Serving tip: whole nuts and thick spoonfuls of nut butter are choking risks for children under four. Spread it thinly on toast or stir it into oatmeal. Choose one with no added sugar or salt.

  9. Whole grain bread

    The base of a thousand lunches. Whole grain bread has more fibre than white bread, and "whole wheat" is not always the same as "whole grain", so read the ingredient list.

    Label check: bread can be a quiet source of sodium. Compare loaves.

  10. Milk or fortified soy beverage

    Calcium, protein and vitamin D. Plain, unsweetened versions are the ones to buy; chocolate and flavoured milks add sugar.

And to drink: water

Canada's food guide makes water the drink of choice at every age. Pop, sports drinks and fruit drinks are the easiest sugar for a child to take in without noticing.

How you offer food matters as much as what

  • Keep offering. Children are more likely to accept a new food the more often they see it. A refusal today is not a verdict.
  • You choose what, they choose how much. Guidance for parents is to offer the food and let the child decide how much of it to eat.
  • Eat together, and let them help. Children who wash, stir and choose are more willing to taste.
  • Sit down to eat. Young children should be seated and supervised while eating, to lower the risk of choking.

A word on allergies

Several foods on this list, including eggs, fish, milk, peanuts, tree nuts, soy and wheat, are common allergens. If your child has a diagnosed allergy, follow your allergist's advice and read every label, every time.
