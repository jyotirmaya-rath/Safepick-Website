Food companies spend a great deal of money on the front of a package. Colours, mascots, words like "natural" and "wholesome": all of it is there to win the three seconds you give a product before it goes in the cart. Canada's food guide tells shoppers to be aware of food marketing for exactly this reason.

The honest information is on the back, in small print. Almost nobody has time to read it for forty products in one trip. But you are already carrying a tool that can.

Think of your phone as a shield, not a screen

A barcode scanner turns the back of the pack into a ten-second check. Point the camera at the barcode and the nutrition table, the ingredient list and the declared allergens are read for you and summed up in one line. You stay in charge of the decision. You just make it with the facts in front of you instead of the advertising.

Four habits that make it work

  1. Scan before it goes in the cart. Not at home, when the choice is already made. The shelf is where a better option is one arm's length away.
  2. Read the reasons, not only the number. "High in added sugar" and "high in sodium" are different problems. Knowing which one applies tells you what to look for in a replacement.
  3. Check the better choices. A scanner that only says no isn't much help. SafePick shows similar products that score higher, so you can swap like for like.
  4. Tell it about your household. Save an allergy, a diet or a goal once, and every scan checks it. A product can score well and still be wrong for your family.

Where to start

Don't try to scan the whole store. Start with the things you buy every single week: the cereal, the bread, the yogurt, the kids' snacks, the pasta sauce. A small improvement in a product you eat two hundred times a year is worth more than a perfect choice you make once.

What a scan can't do

  • It reads the label, not the food. If the data on file is incomplete or out of date, the result is too. SafePick says so when data is missing instead of guessing.
  • It doesn't replace the pack for allergies. Recipes change and "may contain" statements matter. Use the scan to narrow down, then read the pack.
  • It isn't medical advice. A score is general information. For diabetes, pregnancy, a child's diet or any medical condition, ask a health professional.
